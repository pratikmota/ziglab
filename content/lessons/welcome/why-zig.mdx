People do not pick Zig because it has the most features. They pick it because **surprises stay rare**.

When a program misbehaves, you want to debug **your** code: the loop, the allocator, the integer width. You do not want to hunt a hidden conversion, a constructor that allocated, or a destructor that ran on the way out. Zig leans toward “what you see is what runs.”

**Comptime** is the distinctive trick. Some Zig runs while the program is compiling. You can generate code, check sizes, and specialize with ordinary-looking functions instead of a second macro language. You will meet a small piece of that later. For now: comptime is still Zig, just earlier.

Zig is also a practical way to keep talking to **C**. Teams with a large C or C++ tree often want a clearer language at the edges without throwing the tree away. Zig is built for that job.

None of this makes Zig “officially better.” It is a tool. For the language itself, go to [ziglang.org](https://ziglang.org/). ZigLab is an independent lab that walks a path in the browser.
