Zig does not treat “a number” as one type. Integers and floats are separate, and integers have a **width**.

**Integers.** `i32` is a signed 32-bit integer. `u8` is an unsigned 8-bit integer (0 through 255). `i64`, `u16`, and the rest follow the same pattern: `i` or `u`, then the bit count. `usize` is the pointer-sized unsigned integer, used for indexes and sizes.

**Comptime integers.** A bare `3` in source is a comptime integer until it has to pick a concrete type — for example when you write `const count: i32 = 3;`. You do not need the details today. Remember that the type is not guessed from “it looks numeric.”

**Floats.** `f32` and `f64` are IEEE floats. You cannot assign a float to an integer slot without being explicit. Zig would rather you notice the conversion than silently drop the fractional part.

**Bool.** `bool` is `true` or `false`. It is not an integer, even though some languages let you add `true` to `1`.

Pick the width you mean. A `u8` that overflows is a different story from a `u32`. The next page is a short safety note about that.
