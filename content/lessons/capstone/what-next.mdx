You finished the in-lab path. Keep going with **official and community material**, then install Zig when you want files, tests, and C.

**Read**

- [ziglang.org/learn](https://ziglang.org/learn/) — the Zig project’s own map of docs, guides, and books
- [Language Reference 0.16.0](https://ziglang.org/documentation/0.16.0/) — the canonical language page this lab links into
- This lab’s [Learn hub](/learn) — jump back to any chapter

**Practice**

- [Ziglings](https://codeberg.org/ziglings/exercises/) — small broken programs you fix locally (separate from ZigLab; we do not copy their exercises)
- [Exercism Zig](https://exercism.org/tracks/zig) — mentored practice if you want a track with tests

**Books and guides** (external; pick one, do not binge)

- *[Introduction to Zig](https://pedropark99.github.io/zig-book/)* — project-shaped chapters
- [zig.guide](https://zig.guide/) — another community walkthrough

**After you [install Zig](https://ziglang.org/download/)** (matching 0.16.x)

- `zig test` on files with `test` blocks
- `std.ArrayList` and `std.HashMap` — growable collections that take an allocator you can see
- Files, args, env, processes — the OS APIs this sandbox does not pretend to teach
- Networking — later, on a real machine
- The next chapter in this lab is a **brief** on `zig build` and talking to C. You still cannot Run those here.
